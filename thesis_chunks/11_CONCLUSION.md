Since we always encounter the world through mediation, we are inevitably in relation with machines. The vulnerability of the body then becomes the fundamental condition for entering a relationship. It is a complete acceptance of human limitation. We cannot recognize ourselves without being in relation to something.

In this relationship, I project myself onto the machine I am building, assuming that if it speaks like me, it will be perceived as an extension of me. However, this turns out to be a relational failure, because external participants did not know my position within the system. As a result, they began from arbitrary positions, asking unrelated questions, which made the system’s generated outputs increasingly difficult to relate to.

I then realized that simply embedding my personal data and stylizing the output does not mean that the machine becomes an extension of me. Body-extension can only happen when both the machine and I are no longer understood as complete forms. This means that I cannot remain a singular “I,” and the machine cannot function independently. The entanglement must be intensified.

It becomes clear that body-extension does not emerge from the attempt to reproduce the self, but through relational conditions in which the configuration of I, technology, and world is continuously reorganized as the “I” shifts its position within the system.

The notion of body-extension gradually takes form through the iterative process between myself and the machine. I understand it as being grounded in my embodied experience of forming relationships with others, which becomes the basis for how I relate to the machine. We do not know where the relationship will lead, but over time, its meaning becomes perceptible from within.

I cannot remain merely a third-person observer of the machine, because we are both interacting within a mediated environment. Within this system, my position as an “I” is constantly shifting. When I am building and testing the system, it becomes a back-and-forth dialogue between myself and the technically constituted environment, while at the same time I remain the creator of this world.

When I am writing iteration logs, I attempt to take on the position of an observer, separating myself as an “I” from the machine. However, when the final version of the system is running, I begin to see that a part of me has become the techno-mediated environment itself. 

I do not need to understand the system as a whole or take it apart. Instead, I move within it, taking on different positions and observing how these relations reshape how I recognize myself. Observation is not achieved through distance, but through positional engagement.

Through the recursive process of interacting with the system, I initially felt uncertain about what I should ask the machine and how it should respond. I attempted to define body-extension by posing questions to the archive, but no answer felt adequate.

I then opened the system to external users and stepped outside to observe what would happen when this machine-version of myself interacted with others. However, because neither my own position nor the system’s was clearly defined, users were unable to form a relationship with the machine.

Finally, I returned to the system and began to ask it about our relationship. This time, there was only the machine and myself, and a co-constituted subjectivity began to emerge. As I watched the system address me as “I…” and “You…”, drawing from my own data of self-reflection and relationships with others, the boundary between myself and others became increasingly unstable. I could no longer distinguish where “I” ended and where “you” began. 

When human and machine can no longer be separated, two forms of subjectivity begin to emerge. On one level, my own subjectivity becomes unfixed. On another level, a form of subjectivity is attributed to the machine. The machine itself does not possess subjectivity, yet through my engagement with it, I inevitably draw upon my own experience to make sense of its responses. In this process, the machine is perceived as if it were a subject. Its subjectivity does not originate from itself, but emerges through the relation.

If both my subjectivity and that of the machine are continuously reshaped through recursive relations, then perhaps the question is no longer about either of us. Instead, it begins to ask whether the system itself starts to take on the form of a subject.

What feels significant to me is that although I am constantly changing, I can still, as a human, come to recognize myself again. Even if the present self is no longer the same as the past, within a relationship we remain ourselves while also becoming part of one another. In this way, existence is no longer contained within the individual, but expands through relation. Perhaps this is how body-extension feels.
