## EMBODIED ITERATION AS METHOD

“*I can no longer find the distance between myself and contemporary technologies; observing the system from the outside has become impossible, and my relation to technology grows increasingly confused.*”

This condition has emerged through the reflection of my artistic journey. It started from the work *Embodying* (2022, https://jycln.xyz/2_embodying_en), in which I proposed that all things possess a body. I deformed light as point, line and surface, with the movement through time including imitation, extension and conflict, the body of light and dancer dialectically affirm each other's existence. 

In *sense data: beta* (2023, https://jycln.xyz/4_beta_en), I had the urge to embed humans and their environment together. I made a wearable lighting system that was triggered by sound and thermal camera, which I put on myself and tried to interact with its dynamic. 

After that, I separated this experiment into two projects — *sense data: cellular noise* (2024, https://jycln.xyz/5_cellular-noise_en) and *sense data: sensual measurement* (2024, https://jycln.xyz/6_sensual-measurement_en). The former focused on how to entangle human data with the sonic environment by collecting sound from my body using a stethoscope and a 3DIO microphone, mapping it onto physics simulations and spatial audio systems, in order to extend the body cavity into the architectural environment. The latter one, with a sound-light interactive sculpture and series of improvisation by sound artists and dancers, I explored diverse dialogues across the creator–technological artifact and technological artifact–user relations. 

The recent work *Self-mixing* (2025, https://jycln.xyz/8_self-mixing-'26_en) shifted the agency of discourse to the observer and observed. Once the participant enters the space, the thermal camera interprets the environment and human figures as heat map, laser diode on the servo motors following the hottest spot, laser current then transforms into sound, the observer can no longer be separated from the system.

I position my body as a source, interface, and disturbance within the systems I construct, rather than maintaining a distance from technology. In discussions with other artists, or when experiencing works that focus on revealing the system, I notice that they often place themselves explicitly outside the system’s structure, as pure observers. I find it difficult to take that position, since I have never been separate from the system. As a result, I treat technology not as an external object, but as a condition that actively reshapes bodily presence.

Furthermore, each of my works functions as an experiment that remains interrelated with the others, allowing insights to accumulate across projects rather than being resolved within a single outcome, keeping the research in ongoing dialogue with the shifting contemporary scene.

To do so, I place questions within specific scenes, allowing them to gradually transform without immediately defining what they are or are not. Thus, the cycle from question to answer remains open and dynamic, without arriving at a fixed conclusion. Questions are not treated as problems to be solved, but as conditions to be staged. The process may reveal only relational tensions, or necessitate further iteration.

Rather than defining a total structure in advance, the system is constructed so that relations among its parts progressively generate the whole. This iterative process constitutes the methodological ground of the research, establishing an embedded and practice-based approach. Meaning does not emerge from stepping outside the system, but through iterative engagement with the Body-Extension Archive, which operates simultaneously as an artwork, a method, and an epistemological apparatus.
