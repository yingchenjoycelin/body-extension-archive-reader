## EMBODIED ITERATION AS METHOD

“*I can no longer find the distance between myself and contemporary technologies; observing the system from the outside has become impossible, and my relation to technology grows increasingly confused.*”

This condition has emerged through the reflection of my artistic journey. It started from the work *Embodying* (2022, https://jycln.xyz/2_embodying_en), in which I proposed that all things possess a body, displacing the body from a human-centered condition.

In *sense data: beta* (2023, https://jycln.xyz/4_beta_en), the possibility of observing the system from the outside collapsed as I became embedded within it.

After that, I branched this experiment into two projects — *sense data: cellular noise* (2024, https://jycln.xyz/5_cellular-noise_en) and *sense data: sensual measurement* (2024, https://jycln.xyz/6_sensual-measurement_en). These works further differentiated the relations between human, machine, and environment within the system, extending the body into the sonic and architectural environment, while exposing shifting relations across creator, technological artifact, and user.

The recent work *Self-mixing* (2025, https://jycln.xyz/8_self-mixing-'26_en) intensified this condition by shifting the agency of discourse to the observer and observed. Once the participant enters the space, separation between observer and system is no longer possible.

I position my body as a source, interface, and disturbance within the systems I construct, rather than maintaining a distance from technology. In discussions with other artists, or when experiencing works that focus on revealing the system, I notice that they often place themselves explicitly outside the system’s structure, as pure observers. I find it difficult to take that position, since I have never been separate from the system. As a result, I treat technology not as an external object, but as a condition that actively reshapes bodily presence.

Furthermore, each of my works functions as an experiment that remains interrelated with the others, allowing insights to accumulate across projects rather than being resolved within a single outcome, keeping the research in ongoing dialogue with the shifting contemporary scene.

To do so, I place questions within specific scenes, allowing them to gradually transform without immediately defining what they are or are not. Thus, the cycle from question to answer remains open and dynamic, without arriving at a fixed conclusion. Questions are not treated as problems to be solved, but as conditions to be staged. The process may reveal only relational tensions, or necessitate further iteration.

Rather than defining a total structure in advance, the system is constructed so that relations among its parts progressively generate the whole. This iterative process constitutes the methodological ground of this research, establishing an embedded and practice-based approach. Meaning does not emerge from stepping outside the system, but through iterative engagement with the Body-Extension Archive, which operates simultaneously as an artwork, a method, and an epistemological apparatus.
