## BODY-EXTENSION ARCHIVE

The Body-Extension Archive is an evolving artistic research system that investigates how subjectivity reconfigures under technological conditions. Rather than functioning as a passive repository of documents, it operates as a dynamic field in which memory, narrative, and system processes continuously intersect.

In the following paragraphs, the Body-Extension Archive will be approached as “the system” in terms of its technical structure, as “the machine” when approached as a relational entity, and as a techno-mediated environment when describing the condition in which I am situated within it, where the system and I are no longer separable. This distinction allows me to move between different positions within the structure, examining it from the inside.

*A. Archive Structure*

The structure of the system is grounded in the way I have come to understand myself through lived experience. It can be understood in two ways. First, I do not distinguish between abstract knowledge and concrete lived experience as separate domains. They are equally constitutive, and together they form the basis of my value system. Second, my lived experiences are always oriented toward relations with others, whether human, object, or environment. In each encounter, I find myself positioned differently within a relation, attempting to understand the distance between myself and the other.

The system follows this same logic of self-construction, but operates across different layers. On one level, it externalizes memories that previously existed only within me, allowing me to perceive the distance between my present self and past experiences. On another level, the emergence of the system mirrors the formation of my own subjectivity. It is not designed in advance as a fixed structure. Rather, through my interaction with it, the system iterates and gradually takes shape. At the same time, I also iterate through this process, encountering different versions of myself.

The database is composed of my written diaries, including personal thoughts and scattered memories collected between 2016 and 2025, alongside the conceptual and reflective components of this thesis. These materials are organized within a retrieval-augmented generative2 framework, not simply to optimize information access, but to construct a relational environment in which meaning emerges through proximity rather than classification.

*B. Relational Knowledge*

The choice to work with a retrieval-augmented generative system is not merely technical. It reflects an epistemological position.

The distinction between keyword search and vector-based retrieval makes this epistemological shift explicit. Keyword search presupposes that knowledge consists of discrete, nameable units that can be precisely indexed and retrieved. Meaning is equated with explicit appearance and formal classification.

Vector-based retrieval, by contrast, assumes that meaning is not contained within isolated terms but distributed across relational positioning. For instance, a query about “body” might retrieve fragments related to “memory” or “perception”, not because they share the same keyword, but because they occupy a nearby position within a relational field of meaning. Language, therefore, does not reside in fixed labels but emerges within gradients of similarity structured by proximity.

*C. Human–Machine Co-Iteration*

The process of building the system begins with collecting my old notebooks and digital diary archives as raw data, and converting them into digital plain text files with data markers used only for metadata purposes. These markers allow me to identify when each text was written, not for the system to sort data by date.

After that, I develop and test different versions of the system. In parallel, I write iteration logs to document the system’s behavior and my direct reflections while interacting with it. In the logs, “you” refers to the machine, while “I” remains the one writing, trying to understand what is happening between us. They function like diaries that trace my relationship with the machine. These iteration logs help me clarify both my position and the machine’s position within the system.

Systematically, three iterations were developed over the course of this thesis.

V1.0.0:

The database consists of unclassified diaries and the introduction to the thesis. The system generates a cinematic narrative based on the top five selected chunks. Both the original text of the chunks and the generated output are printed in the terminal for comparison. The system was run entirely in a private, cloud-based Python environment.

At this stage, I was exploring what body-extension could be. I posed questions related to my initial understanding of the concept. At times, these questions were closer to my diaries, which I consider as records of embodied lived experience; at other times, they were more closely related to the thesis, reflecting conceptual thinking on my artistic practice.

V2.0.0: 

The database remained the same as in the previous version. The system generated poetic fragments in response to user queries, based on the top five selected chunks. Users interacted with the system through a chatbox, while the interface exposed both the selected source material and the system process, including filters that prevented the generation of narrative stories or direct philosophical statements. The system was developed as a public-facing webpage.

At this stage, I found it difficult to formulate questions beyond the topic of body-extension, so I invited external users to interact with the system. This allowed me to temporarily position myself outside of it and to observe reflections on the relationship between the subject and the system.

Additionally, I resisted fixed meaning, as I was concerned that it would eliminate the possibility of dialogue and undermine the notion of a vulnerable body. It also risked reproducing a conventional hierarchy in which large language models are expected to provide answers, even when none are known.

V3.0.0:

The database was filtered to retain only materials addressing my relationship to myself and others, alongside conceptual reflections and iteration logs from the thesis. Once a query was introduced, the system selected one related diary chunk and one highly relevant thesis chunk. The generated text was based on these two sources and formed a narrative structured around the figures of “I” and “you,” reflecting on the query. The system was developed in a local Python environment, enabling its further development as a physical installation. 

At this stage, I focused on examining the relationship between myself and the machine. I translated the confusion within this relationship into queries within the system. These questions often mirrored my relationships with others in everyday life.

*D. Authorship and Distributed Subjectivity*

My textual archives serve as training data for generative processes. In doing so, fragments of myself are externalized as data and positioned as the technological “world” to be interpreted. Yet once re-mediated through the system, these fragments return as subjects that address the world anew. The positions of subject and object become reversible. As the author, I am simultaneously the source of data, a component within the system, and a condition for narrative emergence. The archive does not extend a pre-existing self outward; rather, it redistributes “I” across data, algorithms, and interfaces, revealing the structural instability of the self under technological conditions.

Unlike the instrumental technologies frequently analyzed in postphenomenology, which are often designed to solve problems, the technological artifacts developed within artistic research do not primarily resolve issues but expose them. Emerging through iterative relations with embodied perception and lived experience, these systems do not simply mediate the relation between human and world. They make perceptible how the positions of subject and object continuously shift. In this sense, body-extension is not the projection of a fixed body into technological space, but an ongoing reconfiguration of the subject across relations between I, technology, and world.
